# Weather & Conditions Deep-Dive — Monday, July 6, 2026 (Seneca SC → Alcoa TN via Highlands / Fontana / Cherohala)

## Bottom line first
**Monday is a classic — but earlier-than-classic — southern-Appalachian pulse-storm day.** The whole route is dry until roughly midday, then thunderstorm probabilities climb steeply from west to east: the far-western NC/TN high country (Fontana–Cherohala) goes to "storms likely" **as early as 12–2pm**, not the textbook 3–5pm. The planned 4:15–5:45pm Cherohala run currently sits in the single wettest cell of the entire day (81–86% hourly PoP at ~4,400–5,400 ft). **Front-load the day: depart ~8:15–8:30am instead of 9:15 and pull every stop ~45–60 min earlier.** The morning waterfall corridor is a guaranteed-dry win; the afternoon is a radar-management exercise. Waterfalls are in **moderate-to-good flow** (Tellico basin got a real soaking July 4 — Bald River will be pumping). **No smoke, no air-quality alerts, and post-holiday-Monday crowds are genuinely low** (July 4 fell on Saturday; the observed holiday was Friday July 3, so Monday is a normal workday).

**Storm character (NWS Greenville-Spartanburg discussion):** marginal severe risk, SBCAPE 1,500–2,500 J/kg with only 10–15 kt shear → **pulse storms whose main hazard is damaging microburst wind gusts** plus torrential brief rain and lightning. These are hit-and-run cells, not organized lines — gaps between cells are real and radar is your friend. NWS Morristown: convection follows the "diurnal pulse… afternoon/evening hours before faltering at night," **fires over the higher terrain first**, valley initiation less certain.

## When to be OFF exposed mountain sections
- **Cherohala Skyway crest (Santeetlah Overlook, 5,390 ft):** ideally traversed **before ~2:00–2:30pm**; after 3pm, assume you're threading between cells with likely in-cloud fog, standing water, and lightning at overlooks. If you're on it during a cell: it's rain/fog/lightning hazard, not a road-closure event — keep moving, skip overlook stops, stay in the car (a car is a safe lightning shelter; a 5,390-ft open overlook on foot is not).
- **US-64 Cullasaja Gorge & Whitewater Falls:** fully safe in the morning plan — dry through at least 2pm.
- **Look Rock (evening):** 61% PoP at 7pm dropping to 47% at 8pm; storms decay near sunset. If a cell is overhead at 7:00, wait it out over dinner in Townsend/Walland — sunset is ~8:50pm, so an 8:00–8:15 Look Rock still works and post-storm light there can be spectacular.
